mGrowthDB is a relational database that provides a structure to store different types of microbial growth data. Our main objective is to serve as a comprehensive resource for scientists interested in studying the growth dynamics of microbial species by providing access to growth projects and studies uploaded by other researchers.

Upload your study

We encourage researchers to authenticate themselves using {{ "https://orcid.org/"|external_link("ORCID") }} and upload their experimental data by following a step-by-step process that ensures it fits a standardized structure. Sharing growth data publicly plays a crucial role in increasing scientific progress and collaboration within the research community. If your data is published in a paper or pre-print, you can provide a DOI to let us pre-fetch some of the metadata for you, including authorship and licensing information. Here are a few example studies that showcase the variety of data in mGrowthDB:

  • SMGDB00000005: An examination of microbial community stable states by {{ "http://msysbiology.com/"|external_link("Faust lab") }} using perturbation experiments.
  • SMGDB00000013: A study by {{ "https://wp.unil.ch/mitrilab/"|external_link("Mitri lab") }} about bacterial degradation of metal working fluids, leading to insights about positive inter-community interactions.
  • SMGDB00000015: An investigation of microbial species relevant to the human respiratory tract and their community dynamics by the {{ "http://www.gorelab.org/"|external_link("Gore lab") }} in MIT.
  • SMGDB00000025: Data on the impact of carbohydrates of human gut bacteria with a focus on C. difficile strains. A collaborative study between {{ "https://gibbons.isbscience.org/"|external_link("Gibbons lab") }} and {{ "https://www.venturellilab.org/"|external_link("Venturelli lab") }}.

Learn more about uploading your data in our documentation or check out our {{ "https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLt4q_h8pw5qfMb5fL-UCSf3SNjhR-Ld4a"|external_link("video tutorial") }}.

Visualize and analyze growth data

  • Visitors can visualize the growth of microbial cultures within or across experiments in any published study. They can also collect data traces from different studies and investigate them in our "Comparison view". If provided, metabolites and pH measurements can be plotted along with cell abundances for additional insights.
  • Study owners can fit selected growth models to their data through the web interface, courtesy of the {{ "https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/growthrates/"|external_link("growthrates R package") }}. Custom model predictions can be uploaded and attached to the data traces for the benefit of other researchers. Growth parameters can be downloaded in CSV format.
  • In case you wish to explore your data without uploading it to a study or simply test the visualization and model-fitting interfaces, you can try the Workspace pages, where you can experiment freely. The uploaded data will be private by default, or you can make it visible and share it with colleagues.
